Bluecut vs Anti-Glare: Which Lens Do You Actually Need?

Shivam Opticals · Eye Care Guide

← Back to Blog

Almost every customer who walks into our Dariyapur or Kasba branch asks some version of the same question: "Bluecut lagwa loon ya anti-glare?" The honest answer is that most people are comparing two things that aren't really competing — they solve different problems, and a lot of people end up wanting both. Here's the simple breakdown.

What Anti-Glare Actually Does

Anti-glare (also called AR — anti-reflective) is a thin coating applied to the lens surface. Its job is purely optical: it cuts down the reflections that bounce off your lens from headlights, tube lights, the sun, and screens. Without it, you're seeing a faint ghost-image of every light source layered on top of whatever you're looking at.

If you drive at night, sit under harsh office lighting, or just want your eyes to look clear in photos instead of showing a white glare patch, anti-glare is the coating doing that work — regardless of whether you use a computer or not.

What Bluecut Actually Does

Bluecut is a different kind of coating (or sometimes built into the lens material itself). It filters out a portion of the high-energy blue-violet light that comes specifically from LED and digital screens — phones, laptops, TVs, tablets. This is the light most linked to digital eye strain, tired eyes by evening, and disturbed sleep when you're on your phone right before bed.

Bluecut does not reduce glare or reflections. A lens can have bluecut and still show you a glare patch under a tube light if it doesn't also have anti-glare.

So Which One Do You Need?

  • You drive often, especially at night, and don't spend much time on screens: Anti-glare alone is usually enough.
  • You're a student or work on a laptop most of the day, but rarely drive at night: Bluecut alone can help, though we still recommend AR since it comes bundled in most lenses today.
  • You do both — screens all day and driving/outdoor glare: This is most people. A combined AR + Bluecut lens covers both without any trade-off.
A quick way to tell if your current glasses have either coating: tilt them under a tube light. If you see almost no reflection, you already have anti-glare. Bluecut is harder to spot by eye — if you're not sure what your current lenses have, bring them in and we'll check for free.

Still Getting Headaches Even With the Right Coating?

Coatings only help if your underlying prescription is still correct. If you're getting headaches by evening despite wearing bluecut or anti-glare glasses, the lens coating usually isn't the problem — an outdated number is.
