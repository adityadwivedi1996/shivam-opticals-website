5 Signs You're Overdue for a Free Eye Test

Shivam Opticals · Eye Care Guide

← Back to Blog

Most people don't wait for an eye test because their eyesight has gotten dramatically worse overnight — it happens slowly, over months, until certain habits quietly become normal. If you've caught yourself doing any of the five things below, it's worth walking in for a free check before it becomes a bigger headache, literally.

1

Headaches that show up by evening

If your head starts hurting after a full day of screen work, reading, or driving — and it happens often, not just once in a while — your eyes are likely straining to compensate for a prescription that's no longer quite right.

2

Holding your phone or book further away

Noticed yourself stretching your arm out a little more than you used to, just to read a message clearly? That's usually one of the earliest, most-ignored signs of a changing prescription.

3

Squinting at signboards, the TV, or a whiteboard

If distant text that used to be easy to read now needs a slight squint to make out, that's your eyes doing extra work they shouldn't have to.

4

Eyes feel tired even without doing anything difficult

General eye fatigue, heaviness, or a burning feeling by the end of the day — even on days without much screen time — often points to uncorrected or under-corrected vision rather than just tiredness.

5

You genuinely can't remember your last eye test

If it's been over a year, or you're not sure when you last had one, that alone is reason enough. Eyes change gradually enough that you often don't notice until someone else points it out — or until you finally get tested.

If it's your child, watch for this instead: sitting unusually close to the TV, holding books very near their face, or frequently complaining they can't copy from the board at school. Kids rarely say "I can't see clearly" — they just adjust their behaviour, so these habits are often the only clue you'll get.

Why "It'll Pass" Isn't the Right Call

A lot of people push through these signs for months, assuming it's just tiredness or stress. The trouble is that uncorrected vision makes your eyes work harder for everything — reading, screens, even walking around in the evening — and that extra strain is exactly what causes the headaches and fatigue in the first place. A five-minute test tells you clearly whether it's your eyes or something else, so you're not guessing.
